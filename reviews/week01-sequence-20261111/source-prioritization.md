## Minimum principle, computation and independent assessment

For a provided finite table `E` of shape `(V,d)` and integer IDs `I` of shape `(B,T)`, require `0≤I<V`. Lookup gives `H[b,t,j]=E[I[b,t],j]`, shape `(B,T,d)`. With the explicitly designated PAD code, let `m[b,t]=1` for a valid position and 0 for PAD. Then

`n[b]=sum_t m[b,t]`, and `mean[b,j]=sum_t m[b,t]*H[b,t,j]/n[b]` when `n[b]>0`.

Explain which axis is reduced and why the mask needs an added singleton vector axis for broadcasting. Count positions by their ID contract, not by whether the looked-up vector is numerically zero. Specify how to report all-PAD input before division. Distinguish PAD from an unknown real token. Do not normalize arbitrary token-ID numbers or treat their arithmetic mean as a representation.

The audit executed independent synthetic arithmetic in memory, without changing files. With V=5, T=4, d=2 and B=2, valid counts `[2,3]` gave means `[[1,2],[4,0]]`; averaging all T positions gave `[[0.5,1],[3,0]]`. Incorrectly excluding a valid zero vector gave `[[1,2],[6,0]]`. Reordering valid tokens changed lookup positions but preserved their mean. Changing only the PAD table row preserved the correctly masked result. Explicit negative/out-of-range and all-PAD checks rejected the supplied counterexamples. These are finite design checks, not official data or student outcomes.

**Final IDs alone cannot detect every wrong mean.** Existing B's queries are `[[1,2],[2,1]]`, gallery `[[2,1],[0,3],[1,1],[2,0]]`, gallery IDs `[90,8,53,17]`. In an independent synthetic padding construction with T=4 and valid counts `[3,2]`, dividing zero-padded sums by T instead of the valid count gives `[[0.75,1.5],[1,0.5]]`. This is a positive row rescaling. Both correct and incorrect queries still select raw-dot IDs `[8,90]` and cosine IDs `[53,90]`; their cosine score matrices coincide. Therefore inspect valid counts, sums and every pooled coordinate against independent hand expectations before checking retrieval. Returning the right ID is insufficient evidence that the new sequence calculation is right.

For a new independent input change V, T, d and values, include repeated IDs and different valid lengths, then require lookup values/shape, mask, counts, sums, means and ID correspondence before running code. Give feedback after saving the first attempt; retest with another input. Preserve required bridge A/B and old main exercises. Do not hand the target query matrix to learners in a way that lets them bypass sequence construction and still pass the final-ID check.

## Placement and unobserved evidence

Week 1 should cover only the small indexing/axis/valid-average prerequisite. Week 2 already treats fit boundaries, time, imputation, categorical handling and metric denominators; do not recreate those full lessons. Weeks 3–5 connect summaries, losses, distances and representations; Week 6 adds learned tensor operations and gradients. Week 8 supplies tokenization, vocabulary/OOV policies, real embeddings, positional information and attention; Week 9 adds actual variable-length audio/multimodal representations. Week 10 handles unfamiliar contracts and inference environments. Masked arithmetic mean is not attention, causal masking, softmax, or a model for chronological reconstruction. Broad D07/D08 and deferred output demands stay open where unfulfilled.

At the inspected state Weeks 3–10 were plans rather than actual notebooks. The plan's old 120-minute class and 5–7-hour Week 1 self-study estimates do not measure the expanded route. Student time, assistance, independent performance, retention and transfer remain unobserved. Reusing one existing path limits duplication but does not prove the workload fits or that students have attained the skill.
