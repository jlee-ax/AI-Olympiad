# Competition-depth review: evidence and limits

This is a supplementary, reviewer-led procedure for KOAI material and its bridge toward the contest. It does not replace K01–K16, prove learner outcomes, or automatically establish exam equivalence. Existing frozen reviews remain historical; applying this procedure to an artifact produces a new review with its own revision, sources, and reviewer. Do not increase the number of pages or difficult terms as a proxy for depth.

## Source and scope first

1. Read the target year's official rules and linked syllabus directly. Record their URLs, publication or version label, access date, exact location, topic and intended theory/practical mode. Record conflicts: the [KOAI 2027 guide](https://kitpa.org/koai/2027) describes all four subjects for the middle-school division, a three-hour written exam (80 multiple-choice and 10 written responses), and a three-hour coding exam (two coding tasks); its [linked syllabus](https://kitpa.org/koai/syllabus) was headed IOAI 2026 when reviewed on 2026-11-10. Do not turn that heading into a 2027 syllabus version.
2. Search for directly accessible official past problems or examples relevant to the selected objective. Record the original URL, exact question number/section, year, division or intended audience, official status (past problem versus sample), read extent, and what the source does *not* establish. If no relevant question or its provenance can be verified, record `uncertain` and the search/access gap; do not infer exam difficulty from a syllabus row alone. Do not reproduce restricted questions: link to the original and make independently authored exercises where needed.
3. The [2026 release notice](https://kitpa.org/files/36) identifies its [PDF](https://kitpa.org/storage/c4016751-0e2c-4548-ab70-ecec994e8f8d.pdf) as reference examples written to a high-school standard, not middle-school past questions; a complete official answer key is not provided (the PDF gives an expected output in its K-NN example) and educational organizations are asked to seek permission for use. It cannot establish the difficulty distribution of the 2027 middle-school exam. The PDF's multiple-choice question 2 concerns a NumPy array's shape, reductions and a reshape/memory assertion; question 6 distinguishes a tensor axis swap from reshape-like operations. Its written question 2 specifies shapes and uses the smaller class label if the K-NN majority vote is tied; it does not specify a rule for all possible distance ties. These are *examples of observable demands*, not a mandate to teach K-NN, PyTorch, or memory internals in week 1.

## Compare actual demands, not topic labels

For each selected objective, inspect the exact official source location and the actual lesson region, exercise, and key. Describe what a learner must *do*, not merely topic names or word counts. For each applicable dimension below, record source and artifact locators, the evidence, and one of `met`, `gap`, or `uncertain` relative to the stated stage. A `met` is a recorded, scoped reviewer judgment, not machine-verified truth. When a dimension is deferred, state the concrete later prerequisite, practice and assessment link, or leave the trajectory uncertain.

- **Representation and invariants:** identify quantities, units, shapes, axes, assumptions and preserved/changed values; translate among a table, equation, diagram and code as relevant.
- **Operation selection and consequences:** choose an operation under a meaningful constraint, predict output and explain why an apparently plausible alternative fails. Simply calling the named function in a supplied example is guided use.
- **Changed condition and boundary:** use a genuinely changed condition, shape, definition or failure case; require reasoning about assumptions, edge cases, errors or a counterexample. Merely changing numbers or column names can be useful early practice but is not by itself evidence of a new reasoning demand. An actual unaided response on a changed input may provide limited evidence of near transfer under the observed conditions.
- **Theoretical mechanism and interpretation:** connect definitions and justified steps to an algorithm and the meaning/limitations of a result. A correct final number, longer derivation, or decorative figure alone does not establish depth.
- **Independent problem solving:** identify what decisions, implementation, checking and explanation remain for the student when examples/answers are hidden, including permitted help. A task design can be inspected; only a real learner's observed work can establish performance.
- **Progression and workload:** identify the appropriate introductory goal, prerequisites, supported practice and a specific path to the larger official demand; distinguish the 120-minute class from separately specified independent work. Check the agreed assessment format as well as minutes: for the pinned plan, the ten-minute written block contains two multiple-choice items, one short written response and feedback. Do not require the entire later contest task in the first session or assert time feasibility without observations.

A **gap** is a specific consequential demand or prerequisite in the chosen trajectory for which the artifact or explicitly linked continuation lacks adequate evidence, with a reason and an actionable object. **Uncertain** is missing/ambiguous source, location, continuation, answer check or observation; it is not a student failure. Mark a scoped introductory objective `met` only if its actual design and stated stage support it; do not label the whole contest standard met because one local activity passes.

### Worked comparison, not an official solution

For week-1 array foundations, a changed-input exercise that repeats axis means demonstrates practice with reductions, but does not by itself assess selecting between reshape and axis exchange. An independently authored bridge can give a 2×3 array, ask learners to compare `.reshape(3, 2)` and `.T` element by element, then state which exchanges axes and why the identical output shape is insufficient. Independently check the answer and offer prerequisite support; keep this bridge distinct from claiming PyTorch or a full contest problem has been mastered. If the lesson or later assessment already supplies an equivalent task, cite its exact location instead of declaring a gap by assumption.

## Carry the review through

- Freeze the baseline artifact hash and compare it against the actual candidate, using the same rubric and source access. Record a minimal falsifiable hypothesis, a relevant baseline weakness, material changes, counterexamples and no-change/rejected findings. Re-run checks affected by edits and preserve prior records.
- Verify numerical answers independently; execute the exact notebook from a clean state when feasible; review problem statements, feedback, help conditions and answer keys. Have a separate reviewer inspect the artifact and original sources, not just a summary. Record reviewer/model and same-family or unavailable-review limits. Challenge this review with a known relevant gap and a neighboring adequate case.
- Bind source → demand → prerequisite → teaching → independent assessment → feedback in the dependency graph. Graph validity covers declared nodes/edges, not missing ideas or semantic truth. Run the supplemental `contest_depth.py` report for explicitly recorded comparisons; it checks records, surfaces recorded gaps and missing evidence, but does not browse, read the notebook, verify source authenticity, or measure difficulty or learning.
- Report separately: source/record status, observed material strengths and gaps, task-design judgment, execution checks, and unobserved learner results. Do not convert absence of a student study into proof that the material is shallow, or use a correct executable notebook as evidence that its competition depth is sufficient.
